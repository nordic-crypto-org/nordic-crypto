[s1 @ 27.9s, story]
The US Department of Justice said on the twelfth of November that a court has authorised the seizure or freezing of more than one point two billion dollars in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance. More than two point seven billion dollars have already been frozen. The FBI attributes the theft to the group it calls TraderTraitor. That is the FBI's attribution. The same release also covers a separate scheme, FaceStealer, which is not a new Binance theft. VG reported the release.

[s2 @ 53.8s, story]
Lithuania's Axiology has received an expanded MiFID authorisation from the Bank of Lithuania, Kaupr reports, citing the company. The authorisation covers receiving, transmitting and executing client orders. Axiology says institutions can subscribe and redeem units in tokenised money-market funds through it. The firm already operates under the EU DLT Pilot Regime.

[s3 @ 75.2s, story]
The Norwegian government will start work on a licensing scheme for data centres. That is a press release on the ninth of November from the Ministry of Digitalisation and Public Governance and the Ministry of Energy, Shifter reports. The government said facilities used for crypto activity will not be treated as socially beneficial. It aims to present the main principles in the autumn of twenty twenty-seven. Statnett has separately paused new grid reservations for data centres.

[s4 @ 100.3s, story]
Norwegian Block Exchange and Aprikos Venture have signed a joint venture for a planned Pakistan exchange called Nayapaisa, Kaupr reports, citing the companies. They have applied to Pakistan's Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority for a No Objection Certificate. That application is a first step. The exchange will not serve customers until both the certificate and a licence are granted. The plan is deposits and withdrawals in Pakistani rupees through licensed Pakistani banks.

[b_se @ 126.1s, section - SWEDEN]
(music/stinger only)

[s5 @ 127.7s, story]
According to Flamman's own investigation, companies and people around Valuno's former chief executive, Daniel Sonesson, have continued to operate in payments, including services used by online casinos. Those links are the paper's findings. Sweden's Economic Crime Authority has charged Sonesson with aggravated insider trading. He denies wrongdoing, according to Svenska Dagbladet. A charge is not a conviction.

[s6 @ 151.5s, story]
Crédit Mutuel Arkéa has confirmed a cyberattack through a service provider's platform, and said the funds may then have been converted into cryptocurrency. That is the bank's statement of the fourth of November, Realtid reports. Blockchain investigator ZachXBT first flagged large unauthorised withdrawals from crypto-exchange accounts. Realtid cites figures of about one hundred and thirty-three million dollars. The bank has not confirmed that amount. It said it could only validate the order of magnitude.

(music/stinger only)

[s7 @ 180.4s, story]
Customer interest in crypto products at Danske Bank and Nordea has been extremely limited, Børsen reports, even though the banks have spoken of strong interest. A staff paper from Danmarks Nationalbank, dated the fifteenth of April twenty twenty-six, not a new November survey, found that household exposure to crypto in Denmark remains low. It noted that the two banks now offer crypto exchange-traded products. Danske Bank said on the eleventh of February that customers on its platform can invest in selected products that track bitcoin and ethereum.

[b_fi @ 208.0s, section - FINLAND]
(music/stinger only)

[s8 @ 209.6s, story]
Yle reports that the Finnish tax office says fewer than one in six crypto traders in Finland declare their trades. That share has not been confirmed in the tax authority's published notes on the new reporting duty. From next year, the authority is due to receive data from Finnish and foreign crypto-asset service providers, according to Vero dot fi.
