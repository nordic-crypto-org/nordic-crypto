Good evening, and welcome to Nordic Crypto. This is issue number three, with the Nordic crypto news from the 12th to the 19th of October, 2026.

[open_b @ 15.0s, tonight]
Tonight: Sweden's central bank asks parliament to prepare a possible digital krona. Norway argues over crypto mining at data centres. And new steps on stablecoins and crypto tax.

[b_se @ 26.5s, section - SWEDEN]
(music/stinger only)

[s1 @ 28.5s, story]
Kaupr reports that the Riksbank has asked the Riksdag to prepare legislation for a possible digital krona. The bank has not decided to introduce one. Any issue would need the Riksdag's consent.

[s2 @ 39.8s, story]
Realtid reports, citing the Financial Times, that Payward, owner of Kraken, is buying Saldo Bank, which offers savings accounts in Sweden. The deal needs approval and is expected in 2027.

[b_no @ 51.7s, section - NORWAY]
(music/stinger only)

[s3 @ 53.7s, story]
Ságat reports that a claim by the data-centre firm Arctic Core, that crypto mining is common in the industry, is now a question in the Storting. The paper had reported that the firm would do a little mining in Kautokeino.

[s4 @ 65.2s, story]
The industry body Norsk Datasenter Industri rejects that claim. Its chief executive tells Ságat that it is not common to start with crypto mining. Part of the article is behind a subscription.

[s5 @ 76.6s, story]
Digi.no reports, from the digitalisation minister's answer to MP Lars Haltbrekken, that fewer than half of Norway's data-centre operators paid corporate tax for 2024. Crypto miners are among the largest taxpayers in that overview.

[s6 @ 90.1s, story]
Ságat also reports that Naturvernforbundet in Kautokeino wants the municipality to withdraw Arctic Core's building permit. The complaint is not due to be handled before November.

[b_more @ 100.0s, section - MORE NEWS]
(music/stinger only)

[s7 @ 102.0s, story]
Finanstilsynet has put EU rules on fines for issuers of significant tokens into Norwegian law. It says the EFTA Surveillance Authority holds that power in the EEA and EFTA states.

[s8 @ 113.3s, story]
Finanswatch reports that a company backed by 37 banks, including Nordic banks, has an authorisation for blockchain-based euro tokens, with a launch expected soon. Further detail is behind a subscription.

[s9 @ 125.4s, story]
Børsen reports that Denmark's government is signalling new crypto tax rules. A model agreed by the authorities and the industry more than a year ago had been left on the shelf.

[s10 @ 134.9s, story]
Investornytt quotes K33: exchange-traded bitcoin products saw net outflows of ten thousand and sixty-four bitcoin in five days, and the price fell below 80 thousand 500 dollars. K33 links that to a wider move from risk. That is K33's assessment.

[s11 @ 151.2s, story]
Yle reports, via Finnish and Turkish media, that Richard Schueler, also known as Richard Heart, has been detained in Istanbul. He is a suspect in aggravated tax fraud, remanded in his absence in 2024. He has not been convicted.
