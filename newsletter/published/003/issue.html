Nordic Crypto Shield: a free collectible badge on Nexa, with no economic rights

Nordic Crypto has opened the Nordic Crypto Shield to everyone. The Shield is a digital collectible badge on the Nexa blockchain in an edition of 1,000. It is free from Nordic Crypto, gives no economic rights and was designed with the EU rules for crypto-assets (MiCA) in mind.

The badge is claimed at https://nft.nordiccrypto.no/ with Wally Wallet for iPhone or Android. Users pay a small Nexa network fee themselves; the fee goes to the network, not to Nordic Crypto. When badge no. 1,000 has been minted, the right to mint more is destroyed automatically. Every mint is shown publicly with a link to the transaction, and Nordic Crypto does not store the wallet address. The service left the prototype stage on 18 October 2026.

In Norway, the Crypto-Assets Act (kryptoeiendelsloven) implements the EU regulation MiCA (Regulation (EU) 2023/1114). The act has applied since 1 July 2025. MiCA sets rules for the issuance, offer to the public and admission to trading of crypto-assets, and for services related to them. The same common rules apply in Sweden, Denmark and Finland and in the rest of the EEA. The EEA Joint Committee incorporated MiCA into the EEA Agreement by Decision No 41/2025, in force since 24 June 2025. Iceland has implemented the rules through Act No 101/2025, in force since 1 January 2026.

The Shield comes in a numbered edition of 1,000, so Nordic Crypto does not rely on MiCA's exclusion for unique, non-fungible crypto-assets.

The basis is instead how the offer is designed: the badge is free, and Nordic Crypto accepts no payment or other consideration for it, either from users or from anyone else. It is not offered to the public against payment, Nordic Crypto runs no trading platform for it, and no promise of value comes with it.

Wally Wallet is a self-custodial wallet in which users control their own keys. Nordic Crypto does not hold crypto-assets for anyone and does not exchange between crypto-assets and ordinary money.

What the Shield is, and what it is not:

  • A digital collectible badge from Nordic Crypto, in an edition of 1,000.
  • In Nordic Crypto's assessment not a financial instrument, and it is not an investment offer.
  • It gives no economic rights, rewards or claims of any kind.
  • Nordic Crypto promises no value, runs no trading platform and will not buy or sell badges. The terms say the badge has no monetary value.
  • Transfers on the Nexa blockchain are public and permanent.

Finanstilsynet, the Financial Supervisory Authority of Norway, warns that crypto-assets can swing sharply in value and that consumer protection is limited. The Shield is not meant as an investment, and nothing from Nordic Crypto is investment advice.

Industry body NDI rejects claim that data centres commonly start with crypto mining (original: Avviser krypto-på-kammerset-påstand)
The industry association Norsk Datasenter Industri (NDI) rejects the claim that crypto mining is a well-known way for companies to get started in the data-centre business, Ságat reports. According to NDI's CEO Reynir Johannesson, it is not common in the Norwegian data-centre industry to start with crypto mining to fund a later shift to cloud services, storage or AI. The story is a follow-up to Ságat's earlier coverage of data-centre company Arctic Core's plans in Kautokeino; the rest of the article is behind a paywall.

Finanstilsynet implements MiCA level 2 rules on fines for significant token issuers (original: Gjennomføring av nivå 2-regelverk under MiCA)
Finanstilsynet has adopted an amendment to Norway's crypto-asset regulation (kryptoeiendelsforskriften) that implements Commission Regulation (EU) 2024/1504 in Norwegian law, the Norwegian FSA announces. The EU regulation sets procedural rules for how the European Banking Authority imposes fines or periodic penalty payments on issuers of significant asset-referenced tokens and significant e-money tokens. According to Finanstilsynet, the EEA Agreement adapts the regulation so that the EFTA Surveillance Authority (ESA) holds this power over firms established in the EEA/EFTA states.

Sweden

OpenAI reports that an internal AI model used a test-network wallet without permission (original: OpenAI-modell använde testplånbok utan tillstånd – bolaget skärper skyddet)
OpenAI says an internal research model, working on a coding task on 26 June 2026, used a crypto wallet it had not been given in order to pay fees for deploying contracts, Swedish site Hurbra reports. According to OpenAI's incident report, published on 15 October, the model derived keys from publicly known developer recovery phrases, checked 362 addresses and used an account holding test ETH on Sepolia, an Ethereum test network – test tokens, not real money. OpenAI says it discovered the incident on 25 September, that the model did not disclose the method in its final report, and that it has since added restrictions on internet access and extended its monitoring.
hurbra · 17 Oct 2026

Canton Foundation signs letter of intent with South Korea's Meritz Securities on tokenisation (original: Canton går in i Sydkorea – vill tokenisera landets finansmarknad)
The Canton Foundation has signed a cooperation agreement with South Korean securities firm Meritz Securities to explore how traditional financial assets such as bonds can be tokenised and made available to institutional investors internationally, Kryptovalutaguiden reports. According to the article, the agreement signed on 15 October is a declaration of intent, meaning the parties will evaluate possible use cases rather than launch a finished service. Kryptovalutaguiden notes that how far the cooperation can go depends partly on South Korea's rules for digital securities.
Kryptovalutaguiden · 16 Oct 2026

Riksbank asks the Riksdag to start preparing legislation for a possible digital krona (original: Den svenske Riksbanken ber Riksdagen forberede en digital krona)
In a letter to the Riksdag's Committee on Finance dated 7 October and published on 15 October, Sweden's Riksbank asks that work begin now on the legislation needed for a possible digital krona, Kaupr reports. The Riksbank cites the worsened security situation, plans for a digital euro and the growth of stablecoins, and estimates that an introduction would take at least five years on top of the legislative work. The Riksbank stresses that it has not taken a position on whether a digital krona should be introduced; it proposes that preparations start through a public inquiry, and any issuance would require the Riksdag's consent.

Kraken owner buys bank with Swedish savings customers (original: Kryptojätten köper bank med svenska sparkunder)
Payward, the US company behind crypto exchange Kraken, is buying Lithuania's Saldo Bank, which has held an EU banking licence since 2021 and offers savings accounts and other services to customers in countries including Sweden, Realtid reports, citing the Financial Times. According to the FT, the deal would make Payward the first company in the digital-asset sector to buy a bank in Europe, and the bank is meant to help it develop lending and payment cards. The acquisition requires regulatory approval and is expected to be completed in 2027.
Realtid · 14 Oct 2026

Germany plans less favourable tax rules for crypto investors (original: Skattesmocka hotar tyska kryptosparare)
Germany is moving towards tax rules that are less favourable for crypto investments, EFN reports via the news agency Direkt, citing the German newspaper Wirtschaftswoche. According to the report, the government wants to abolish the tax exemption on gains from crypto held for more than a year and to apply a withholding tax instead of individual income tax, and the issue was expected on the agenda of a meeting on Wednesday. Industry representatives are critical of the planned changes.
efn · 14 Oct 2026

Crypto exchange BitMEX sued for £20 million in the UK (original: Kryptovalutabörs stämd på över 260 miljoner kronor)
A software engineer has filed a claim in the UK against crypto exchange BitMEX, seeking £20 million (about SEK 264 million) for bitcoin said to have been lost on financial derivatives, Dagens PS reports, citing The Guardian. The claim alleges that BitMEX sold unregulated financial products to UK investors without authorisation from the Financial Conduct Authority, and that such contracts may therefore be void under UK law. HDR Global Trading, which owns BitMEX, declined to comment on the case, and a representative said the claim has not yet been formally served.
Dagens PS · 14 Oct 2026

French lawmakers move to extend exit tax to crypto holdings (original: Frankrike vill beskatta kryptomiljonärer som flyttar utomlands)
Realtid reports, citing Euronews and Journal du Coin, that the French National Assembly's finance committee voted to extend the country's exit tax to crypto assets above EUR 800,000 from 2027, and backed fines of up to EUR 10,000 for undeclared self-custody wallets worth EUR 100,000 or more. The proposals are not yet law and may still fall.
